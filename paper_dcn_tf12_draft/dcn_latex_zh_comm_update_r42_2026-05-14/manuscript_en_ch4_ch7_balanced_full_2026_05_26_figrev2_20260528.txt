Abstract
Four-vehicle rigid-payload transport couples centroid tracking, distance regulation, and payload-force safety under high curvature, communication
delay/dropout, and actuator degradation. This paper proposes NR-KDCC, a network-resilient Koopman delay-compensated consensus controller
with a stability-projected bilinear Koopman predictor inside a link-quality-aware model predictive control (MPC) loop. Against a task-aligned adap-
tive Koopman embedding (AKE) baseline derived from Singh et al.[1], paired n = 20 tests lower lateral root-mean-square error (RMSE)/maximum
connection utilization from 0.0453/0.2999 to 0.0408/0.0513 under mixed fault/noise and from 0.0519/0.3757 to 0.0424/0.0452 under high commu-
nication noise. In a revised 5 m/s high-delay hairpin diagnostic, NR-KDCC-HC lowers centroid lateral RMSE from 0.2989 to 0.0851 relative to
degraded AKE-M, while full NR-KDCC lowers the 0.10 s moving-mean resultant-force peak from 6240.14 N to 4373.90 N relative to NR-KDCC-
HC. The results indicate that Koopman prediction benefits network-degraded cooperative transport only when link quality, connection geometry,
and payload-force risk enter MPC, and that payload protection creates a bounded tracking–force trade-off.

Keywords: Cooperative transport, Koopman operator, bilinear prediction, model predictive control, networked control,
delay/dropout, fault-tolerant control, Lyapunov certificate

5.7. Stability-Certificate Statistics
    Table 8 shows that high communication noise and mixed fault/noise
have positive aggregate margins and certificate ok ratio 1.0, whereas
nominal clean and single fault contain negative worst-case margins. The
corresponding mode-wise logs further show positive margins for the
nominal and FTC-flagged steps in the communication-stress scenarios.
    The difference arises because an aggregate minimum margin is sen-
sitive to short numerical transients, switching instants, and local viola-
tions in non-stress cases. The manuscript therefore uses the certificate-
verified practical ISS/UUB formulation under a compact operating re-
gion, finite-density certificate failures, and fallback feasibility, rather
than claiming strict global stability.
