I. INTRODUCTION
A
UTONOMOUS vehicles (AVs) have garnered increasing
attention in recent years due to their potential to allevi-
ate humans from the dangers of driving in complex scenar-
ios, as well as mitigate various trafﬁc congestion and acci-
dents stemming from human errors [1], [2]. Within a complete
autonomous driving framework, path tracking control servers as
the bottom module and ﬁnal link, whose performance is crucial
for ensuring driving safety, reliability, and integrity, particularly
under uncertainties and external disturbances.
Path tracking control is typically formulated as a two-
stage process: principled system modeling and controller
design. Numerous studies within this framework highlight
the importance of an accurate model, as it enables reliable
state prediction and directly affects the performance of widely
used methods such as MPC and adaptive control [3], [4], [5].
However, principled vehicle models are usually simpliﬁed
through
approximations
(e.g.,
bicycle
or
reduced-order
dynamic models), making it difﬁcult to capture strongly
nonlinear behaviors [6], [7]. These models are also sensitive to
noise and parameter variations, and their prediction accuracy
degrades signiﬁcantly once the vehicle state deviates from
the nominal linearization regime. Such limitations become
particularly prominent in AV path-tracking applications, where
inherent nonlinearities and strong couplings—such as tire–road
interactions,
load
transfer,
and
actuator
nonlinearities—
frequently drive the system into highly nonlinear domains [8].
Although fully model-free alternatives based on reinforcement
learning (RL) have been explored [9], [10], they often suffer
from
limited
interpretability,
poor
generalization
across
diverse scenarios, and the lack of explicit constraint handling
needed for safety-critical tasks. As a result, the modeling–
controller paradigm remains the dominant approach. The
central challenge, however, lies in enhancing model ﬁdelity
under nonlinear conditions while ensuring robust control
performance, which is key to achieving high-precision path
tracking for AVs.
